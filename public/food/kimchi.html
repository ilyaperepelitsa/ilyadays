Napa cabbage kimchi

A Korean cookbook recipe: the cabbage is brined, salted for 12 hours under a weight, rinsed, then filled leaf by leaf from the middle out with a gochugaru filling and wrapped in its own outer leaves. Your additions go into the filling: dried baby anchovies and sakura shrimp chopped with a knife, and pear juice squeezed through cheesecloth.

4 servings · 1 large jar~15 h (12 h salting) + 5–7 days in the fridge

In winter the first salting is 12 h instead of 7 h. Then 5–7 days in the fridge before serving.

Step by step

  1. 1Brine and halve

    ~15 min

    For this step

    • Napa cabbage1 head (~2.5 kg)
    • Coarse salt, for the brine1 cup (200 ml)
    • Water2 l
    • Stir 1 cup of coarse salt into 2 l of water in a bowl big enough to hold half a cabbage.
    • Peel off the outer leaves and keep them — they wrap the kimchi in step 6.
    • Cut the cabbage in half lengthwise and dunk the halves in the brine.

  2. 2Salt and press

    12 h (17 h in winter)

    For this step

    • Coarse salt, between the leaves1 cup (200 ml)
    • Take the halves out and work the second cup of coarse salt in between the leaves, mostly onto the thick white stems.
    • Leave them cut faces up for 7 hours (12 in winter), then flip them face down for 5 hours more.
    • Keep weight on them throughout — a heavy plate or board, or a bag of water — so the salt gets in evenly.

  3. 3Rinse and drain

    ~1 h
    • Wash the salt off under cold water, opening the leaves so it reaches inside.
    • Stand the halves in a colander and let them drip for about an hour.

  4. 4Prepare the filling

    ~25 min

    For this step

    • Daikon200 g
    • Green onions50 g
    • Garlic2 tbsp
    • Ginger juice1 tbsp
    • Asian pear½ (~150 g)
    • Slice the daikon into thin 0.3 cm matchsticks; cut the green onions into 3 cm pieces.
    • Your additions — chop finely with a knife:
      • dried baby anchovies a small handful (~10 g) — enough to taste a little
      • dried sakura shrimp a small handful (~5–10 g) — the same idea
    • Crush or grate the garlic — a ceramic grater is best, a fine metal one works too.
    • Grate the ginger and squeeze it through cheesecloth for the ginger juice.
    • Grate the Asian pear, put it in cheesecloth and squeeze out the juice — the juice is what you want.

  5. 5Mix the filling

    ~5 min + 30 min
    • Gloves on. Mix everything from step 4 with:
      • gochugaru (Korean chilli powder) 1 cup (200 ml)
      • salted anchovy sauce 100 ml
      • salted shrimp sauce 3 tbsp
      • sugar 4 tbsp
      • kelp (kombu) stock 100 ml
    • Let it stand about 30 minutes so the flavours come together.

  6. 6Fill, wrap and pack

    ~40 min, then 5–7 days
    • Start at the middle: lift the innermost leaf next to the core and smear filling over it right down to the core.
    • Lay it back, lift the next leaf out from the middle and repeat — work outward until every leaf is coated.
    • Fold the outer leaves you saved around each half like a parcel, so none of the filling escapes.
    • Press the parcels into an airtight jar and give them 5–7 days in the fridge before eating.

Source

  • Based on the recipe in Easy Korean Cooking (cookbook), with your additions: dried baby anchovies, sakura shrimp and pear juice.
  • The amounts of your additions are a starting guess — adjust them next batch.

Fish sauce instead of the Korean sauces

If you can’t get salted anchovy or salted shrimp sauce, fish sauce stands in for either — but the Korean versions are worth hunting down.

Coarse salt

Use coarse salt for both the brine and the leaves — fine table salt is saltier by volume and lands unevenly.

Kelp stock

Kombu stock — see shiitake-kombu dashi.

See also kkakdugi.
