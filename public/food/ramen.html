Creamy white ramen (tonkotsu-style paitan)

The creamy white one, modelled on Momofuku’s ramen. The broth starts as a pot of kombu–shiitake dashi, then runs through three bone stages in the same pot: chicken with onion, then pork with ginger and garlic, then beef — 6–8 hours each, lifting every batch out with a spider before the next goes in: bring each stage up on medium heat, then let it go low for the hours. Chashu is pork belly rolled up skin side out, tied, marinated ~12 hours and roasted hot then low; a whole soy-seared octopus tentacle goes on top.

~3 l broth · 8 bowls~2 days (broth 18–24 h), mostly hands-off

Day 1, morning: roll, tie and marinate the chashu (~12 h) and make the dashi. The broth’s three 6–8 h stages run through the night; the chashu roasts in the evening and chills overnight until firm. Day 2: strain, slice, sear, build. Broth, chashu, tare and octopus all keep — see Make ahead & store — so later bowls are just the last few bars.

Step by step

  1. Broth

  2. 1Make the dashi

    ~1 h

    For this step

    • Kombu-shiitake dashi~6 l — see recipe
    • Start with a big pot of kombu–shiitake dashi — about 6 litres, in the stockpot the broth will cook in.
    • Everything after this cooks in that same liquid.

  3. 2Blanch the bones

    high10–15 min

    For this step

    • Pork knee / knuckle (trotters)1–2 knees
    • Chicken bones (drumstick bones, backs)enough to fill the pot about halfway
    • Beef neck bonesabout as much as the chicken
    • Put all the bones in a pot, cover with cold water, bring to a boil and boil hard for 10–15 minutes.
    • Lots of grey scum comes up — that’s the point.
    • Drain everything into the sink.

  4. 3Scrub and rinse

    ~15 min
    • Rinse the bones under cold water and scrub off dark bits, clotted blood and scum (the beef neck bones have the most).
    • This is what keeps the finished broth white rather than grey.
    • Keep the pork and beef bones in the fridge until their stage.

  5. 4Stage 1 — chicken + onion, 6–8 hours

    medium, then low6–8 h

    For this step

    • Chicken bones (drumstick bones, backs)enough to fill the pot about halfway
    • Boiling water for topping upas needed
    • Into the dashi:
      • chicken bones
      • onion 1
    • Bring it up on medium heat, then turn it low and let it simmer, lid ajar.
    • Top up with boiling water whenever the bones start to poke out; scrape the bottom now and then so nothing catches.

  6. 5Stage 2 — pork + ginger + garlic, 6–8 hours

    medium, then low6–8 h
    • Lift the chicken bones and onion out with the spider strainer — the broth stays in the pot.
    • Add:
      • pork knee / knuckle (trotters) 1–2 knees
      • ginger for the broth 30 g
      • garlic head 1
    • Back up on medium, then low again, topping up as before. The broth turns cloudy and richer.

  7. 6Stage 3 — beef, 6–8 hours

    medium, then low6–8 h
    • Lift the pork bones, ginger and garlic out with the spider.
    • Add:
      • beef neck bones about as much as the chicken
    • Medium to bring it up, then low again, topping up as needed — by the end it’s rich and cloudy.

  8. 7Strain

    ~20 min
    • Lift the beef bones out with the spider, then pour the broth through a fine sieve.
    • You want about 3 litres of opaque, white broth that lightly coats a spoon; if it’s thin, boil it down hard.

  9. Chashu

  10. 8Roll, tie and marinate the belly

    ~12 h

    For this step

    • Pork belly1–1.2 kg
    • Mix until the sugar dissolves:
      • soy sauce 200 ml
      • mirin 100 ml
      • sake 100 ml
      • brown sugar 60 g
      • water 200 ml
    • Add:
      • garlic 4 cloves
      • ginger 20 g
      • spring onion greens a handful
    • Roll the belly up tightly from a long side, skin side out, and tie it with kitchen string every ~2 cm.
    • Put the roll in a snug dish, turn it in the marinade and leave it in the fridge for about 12 hours, turning a few times.

  11. 9Roast hot

    oven 230 °C~1 h
    • Seam side down in the dish with its marinade, uncovered, into a very hot oven.
    • After 30 minutes, baste it with the juices and give it another 30 minutes, until the outside is deeply browned.

  12. 10Then low until tender

    oven 120 °C1–1½ h

    Turn the oven down and keep roasting, basting now and then, until a skewer slides into the middle of the roll with no resistance.

  13. 11Chill until firm, untie, slice

    chill overnight
    • Lift the roll out (keep the liquid for tare).
    • Let it cool, then wrap it and refrigerate — overnight is best — until it’s cold and firm.
    • Firm pork slices cleanly.
    • Cut and pull off the string, then slice into rounds about 5 mm thick while it’s still cold.

  14. Tare

  15. 12Reduce the marinade into tare

    medium~20 min

    For this step

    • Chashu marinade, strained and reduced~250 ml
    • Suggestion — your notes don’t mention a tare.
    • Strain the cooking liquid, skim the fat off (keep it: a spoon on top of the bowl is great) and simmer to reduce by about a third.
    • Taste: it should be very salty and sweet on its own — it seasons the plain broth.

  16. Octopus

  17. 13Sous vide the tentacle

    water bath 77 °C~5 h

    For this step

    • Octopus tentacles (large octopus)1 per bowl
    • Rinse the tentacles (if raw, rub with salt and rinse to remove slime).
    • Seal each whole tentacle in its own vacuum or zip bag, air pressed out, and cook it in the water bath at around 77 °C for about 5 h.

  18. 14Pat dry — keep it whole

    ~10 min
    • Lift the tentacles out of the bags and pat them dry.
    • Keep each tentacle whole — no slicing; it goes on the bowl in one piece.

  19. Noodles & assembly

  20. 15Sear chashu; sear the octopus with soy

    high~3 min

    For this step

    • Chashu2–3 slices
    • Octopus1 whole tentacle
    • Oil for searing the octopusa little
    • Soy sauce for searing the octopusa splash
    • Right before serving, sear the chashu slices in a very hot dry pan for 30–60 seconds a side (or hit them with a torch) until caramelised at the edges.
    • Sear each whole tentacle in a hot pan with a little oil, then add a splash of soy sauce and turn it until it’s glazed and charred at the edges.

  21. 16Warm the bowls

    2 min

    Fill the bowls with boiling water while everything else comes together; tip it out just before building.

  22. 17Tare, then broth

    1 min

    For this step

    • Tare2–3 tbsp
    • Broth350 ml
    • 2–3 tablespoons of tare into each bowl, then 350 ml of boiling broth.
    • Whisk briefly to blend and froth it.

  23. 18Cook the noodles

    high1–2 min

    For this step

    • Fresh alkaline ramen noodles (thin)120–140 g — homemade: alkaline ramen noodles (baked baking soda)
    • Cook the fresh alkaline noodles (homemade: alkaline ramen noodles, made with baked baking soda) in a big pot of boiling unsalted water — thin fresh noodles usually take 1–2 min.
    • Drain hard — any water dilutes the broth.

  24. 19Top and serve

    • Fold the noodles into the broth.
    • Top with:
      • chashu 2–3 slices
      • octopus 1 whole tentacle
      • spring onion sliced
      • nori 1 piece
      • toasted sesame a pinch
      • soft-boiled egg ½
    • The tentacle goes on whole, in one piece. Eat straight away.

  25. Make ahead & store

  26. 20Make ahead & store (optional)

    • Chashu:
      • vacuum-seal the slices one by one, so you can take out exactly what you need
    • Octopus:
      • vacuum-seal the tentacles, still whole
    • Broth:
      • strain it through cheesecloth
      • cool it, then refrigerate for a night (~24 h) until it sets into a jelly
      • spoon or cut the jelly into silicone molds and freeze it into large cubes
      • pop the cubes out and wrap each one in aluminium foil — they store very well

Amounts

  • Bones are by eye, the way you do it: chicken to about half the pot, 1–2 pork knees, beef about the same as the chicken or a bit more.
  • Dashi volume, marinade and per-bowl amounts are starting points (one whole octopus tentacle per bowl is assumed; the octopus sous vide at 77 °C for ~5 h is assumed too — confirm).
  • Adjust to what you did.

Momofuku

  • Inspired by Momofuku’s ramen (David Chang): like theirs, the broth starts from kombu–shiitake dashi — but no bacon, and the bones go in three separate 6–8 h stages (chicken + onion, pork + ginger + garlic, beef).
  • The chashu oven (very hot to colour, then low until tender) follows their pork belly, which is a flat slab; a rolled belly is thicker, so the low stage may need longer — temperatures and times are assumed, confirm.

Clear vs creamy

  • If the broth stays thin and see-through, the boil wasn’t hard enough — crank it and reduce.
  • Knuckle/trotter is where most of the body comes from.

Keeping

In the fridge, chashu, tare and octopus keep 4–5 days; for longer, see Make ahead & store.
