Japanese basics

Niboshi–bonito dashi

Made with the bigger niboshi (dried anchovies), not the tiny baby ones — heads and guts pulled out so the dashi stays sweet instead of bitter. Katsuobushi (bonito flakes) steeped at the end rounds it out. This is the one for oyakodon and tamagoyaki.

1 litre~1 h (incl. 30 min soak)
IngredientsMise en placePrep orderSteps

Ingredients

Dashi

  • Water1 l
  • Niboshi (larger dried anchovies)20 g (about 15–20 fish)
  • Katsuobushi (bonito flakes)15–20 g — see note
  • Kombu5–10 g, optional

Equipment

  • Saucepan
  • Fine sieve (or one lined with a paper towel)
  • Spoon or ladle for skimming

Soaking overnight in the fridge instead of 30 minutes gives a rounder dashi.

Step by step

  1. 1Clean the niboshi

    ~8 min

    For this step

    • Niboshi (larger dried anchovies)20 g
    • Snap off each head, split the belly with your thumbnail and pull out the dark guts.
    • Both make the dashi bitter.
    • Small niboshi can go in whole; for the bigger ones, this step matters.

  2. 2Soak

    30 min+

    For this step

    • Water1 l
    • Kombu5–10 g
    • Put the cleaned niboshi (and the kombu, if using) in a saucepan with 1 litre of cold water.
    • Soak at least 30 minutes, or overnight in the fridge.

  3. 3Heat slowly, kombu out

    medium-low~10 min
    • Bring up to temperature slowly over medium-low heat.
    • If you used kombu, lift it out as soon as small bubbles appear — before it boils.

  4. 4Simmer and skim

    low5–10 min

    Let the niboshi simmer gently for 5–10 minutes, skimming off any foam.

  5. 5Steep the bonito

    heat off2 min

    For this step

    • Katsuobushi (bonito flakes)15–20 g — see note

    Turn off the heat, scatter in the katsuobushi and leave it for about 2 minutes, until the flakes sink.

  6. 6Strain — don’t squeeze

    2 min
    • Pour through a fine sieve.
    • Don’t press the flakes: squeezing makes the dashi cloudy and fishy.

Bonito or not?

  • You weren’t sure whether bonito went into yours.
  • Niboshi plus katsuobushi is the standard combination; for a pure niboshi dashi, skip step 5 — it’s lighter and more anchovy-forward.

Amounts

20 g niboshi and 15–20 g katsuobushi per litre are starting points, not your measured amounts.

Storage

Fridge 3–4 days, or freeze in ice-cube trays.
