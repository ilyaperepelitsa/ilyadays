Ginger scallion noodles (gentler)

Momofuku’s ginger scallion sauce is just scallions, ginger, oil and a little seasoning stirred together raw — and their amount of ginger is hot and hard on the palate. This version keeps the idea but makes it softer: half the ginger, a bit more scallion and oil, the oil poured over hot to mellow the raw ginger, and a pinch of sugar and a drop of sesame oil to round it off. Best with the baked-soda ramen noodles.

4 bowls (about 1½ cups sauce)~30 min

Step by step

  1. 1Slice the scallions

    ~5 min

    For this step

    • Scallions3 cups thinly sliced
    • Slice the whites and light-green parts thinly (a little of the dark green is fine) — about 3 cups.
    • Put them in the heatproof bowl.

  2. 2Mince the ginger — less than you think

    ~5 min

    For this step

    • Fresh ginger¼ cup finely minced
    • Peel with the edge of a spoon, slice thinly, cut into matchsticks, then mince finely — smaller bits bite less.
    • Measure ¼ cup, no more, and add it to the scallions.

  3. 3Pour hot oil over

    medium-high~4 min

    For this step

    • Neutral oil (grapeseed)½ cup
    • Heat the oil in a small saucepan until it shimmers (about 170 °C — a scallion slice dropped in sizzles hard).
    • Pour it over the scallions and ginger. It sizzles and bubbles up — this takes the raw burn off the ginger.
    • Stir once.

  4. 4Season and rest

    15–20 min
    • Stir in:
      • light soy sauce (usukuchi) 2 tsp
      • sherry vinegar ¾ tsp
      • sugar 1 tsp
      • toasted sesame oil ½ tsp
      • salt ½ tsp
    • Leave it 15–20 minutes for the flavours to settle, then taste and adjust the salt.
    • It keeps 2 days in the fridge; stir before using.

  5. 5Cook the noodles

    high~3 min

    For this step

    • Ramen noodles4 portions (~520 g fresh)

    Boil the noodles (homemade: 1½–2 minutes; bought: as the packet says), drain well and shake off the water.

  6. 6Toss and top

    2 min
    • Toss each bowl of hot noodles with 5–6 tablespoons of sauce until every strand is coated.
    • Top with a couple of:
      • pickled cucumber a few slices
      • toasted nori 1 sheet
      • extra sliced scallion a pinch

Original vs this version

  • Momofuku’s original, stirred together raw:
    • 2½ cups sliced scallions
    • ½ cup minced ginger
    • ⅓ cup grapeseed oil
    • 1½ tsp usukuchi
    • ¾ tsp sherry vinegar
    • ¾ tsp kosher salt
  • This version:
    • ginger halved to ¼ cup, minced finer
    • scallions up to 3 cups
    • oil up to ½ cup, poured over hot
    • soy up to 2 tsp, salt down to ½ tsp
    • added: 1 tsp sugar and ½ tsp toasted sesame oil

If it’s still too sharp

  • Cut the ginger to 2 tablespoons.
  • Blanch the minced ginger in boiling water for 30 seconds and drain it before the hot oil goes on.
  • Let the sauce sit an hour before using — it keeps mellowing.
  • Add more scallion rather than more seasoning.

Toppings

  • Momofuku serves these with quick pickles, pan-roasted cauliflower, bamboo shoots and nori.
  • The cucumber pickle works well as the pickle.

Based on

Adapted from Momofuku’s ginger scallion noodles (David Chang and Peter Meehan, Momofuku, 2009), made gentler and written in my own words.
