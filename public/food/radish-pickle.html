Pickled radishes

Unlike the cucumbers, which get a small dressing-style marinade, the radishes soak in a large amount of liquid: cut, salt briefly, then leave it fully submerged in a sweet-vinegar brine (amazu) in the fridge. Pick your vinegar below and the amounts update.

1 jar (~500 g radishes)~15 min + 6 h to days

Step by step

  1. 1Trim and cut

    ~6 min

    For this step

    • Red radishes500 g
    • Cut off the leaves and the root tips.
    • Halve the radishes, or quarter the big ones, so the pieces are about the same size.

  2. 2Salt, then rinse

    20–30 min

    For this step

    • Salt, for drawing out water1 tsp
    • Toss the cut radishes with 1 tsp salt and leave 20–30 minutes — they give off juice.
    • Rinse off the salt and the juice under cold water and drain well. They’ll stay crunchy and take up the brine better.

  3. 3Make the brine

    low~4 min + cooling
    • Warm just until the sugar dissolves (mirin only if using):
      • rice vinegar 150 ml
      • water 150 ml +
      • sugar 60 g (5 tbsp)
      • salt 1 tsp
      • mirin 2 tbsp
    • Don’t boil it. Let it cool.

  4. 4Into the jar

    ~3 min

    For this step

    • Fresh ginger, peeler stripsvery little: about 1 strip per 2–3 radishes
    • Pack the radishes into the jar.
    • Tuck in:
      • fresh ginger, a few peeler strips
      • kombu a 5 cm square
      • dried chilli 1
      • yuzu or lemon peel a strip
    • Pour over the cooled brine until everything is fully submerged.

  5. 5Wait

    6 h – 2 weeks
    • Lid on, into the fridge.
    • Good after about 6 hours, best after 2–3 days.
    • Keeps about 2 weeks; always lift pieces out with clean chopsticks.

  6. 6Serve

    Serve chilled as a side with rice, alongside karaage or in a bento.

Or use the cucumber marinade

Matched to rice vinegar’s ~4.5% acidity for the brine. Non-rice vinegars are sharper and less rounded, hence the extra sugar — taste and adjust.

Reading Korean vinegar labels

  • 양조식초 (brewed vinegar, ~6–7%) is the one the 7% row means.
  • 2배식초 / 3배식초 are double / triple strength — use half / a third of the 7% amount and make up the rest with water.
  • 빙초산 is glacial acetic acid (~99%): never use it as-is.

Amounts

These are starting points for a sweet-sour amazu brine — adjust the sugar to taste on the next batch.

Ginger or kombu?

  • Just a hint of ginger — shave it thin with a vegetable peeler.
  • Kombu adds a little savoury depth.
  • Use both, or leave the kombu out.

Pink brine

The red skins colour the brine and the white flesh pink over a day or two — that’s normal.
