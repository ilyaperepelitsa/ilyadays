Stoic Constitution

Draft · Marcus Aurelius, in a new translation from the Greek

The articles so far

All 418 passages of the Meditations were grouped by meaning into chapters and sections. Here each section, in order, is fused into an article, one chapter at a time. An article makes one statement, then looks at it from five fixed sides: why, in action, practice, when it breaks, and if I doubt it. On a wide screen the five sides stand next to one another as columns. Under the words runs one coloured line for each passage that says them: two lines, two passages. A colour is the same passage in the sentence, in the chips below it and on the map of vectors beside the statement. Purple marks the editor’s own work. Other authors can be added from the Voices bar at the bottom of the screen, to see what each article gains; the first is Seneca. Hover for the exact quotes. Click or tap to read the passages next to what follows from each.

Voices

1 · From the whole book to topics

How the topics were found

Each passage was turned into a vector of numbers that stands for its meaning. Passages close to one another were merged step by step into a tree. Cutting the tree high gives chapters; cutting each chapter again gives sections. The names are the editor’s, written after reading.

3 · Your call

Your review

Every part of an article has a small Verdict button in its corner: the statement, each claim, each quote’s conclusion, each thing an added author brings, and each clause an added author appends to a sentence (tap the clause to see its button). The whole article is judged under its card. Revise and Drop take a note. Everything is saved in this browser only, so copy it into a chat or download it when you are done.

The editor’s decisions and the Verdict buttons are hidden while you read; switch them back on at the top of the page.
