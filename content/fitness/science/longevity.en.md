Three physical traits predict how long and how well people live, and all three respond to training: aerobic capacity (VO2max), muscle strength and mass, and bone strength. They fade quietly. In healthy adults followed for years, VO2max fell 3–6 % per decade in the twenties and thirties and more than 20 % per decade from the seventies on [1].

> **In short:** aerobic fitness is the strongest modifiable predictor of survival, strength and muscle come next, and bone responds only to heavy load where it is applied. The weekly minimum is three lifting sessions, about 150 minutes of cardio with one hard interval session, carries and back extensions twice a week and 8,000 steps a day. An upper-body program does nothing for the hips, so leg and impact work has to be added.

## Aerobic fitness is the strongest signal

- Among 122,007 patients who did a treadmill test, the least fit quarter had five times the mortality of the fittest group (hazard ratio 5.04). Below-average fitness carried a hazard ratio of 1.41 against above-average, as large as smoking in the same cohort [2].
- Among 750,302 US veterans aged 30–95, the least fit fifth had four times the risk of the extremely fit; the lowest risk sat near 14 METs, with no upturn at the top [3].
- Each extra MET (3.5 mL/kg/min) is associated with about 13 % lower all-cause mortality [4]; an overview of 199 cohorts puts it at 11–17 % [5].

These are observations. Fitness is partly inherited, and illness lowers it. But men who went from unfit to fit between two tests five years apart had 44 % lower mortality than men who stayed unfit [6]. The one five-year trial in older adults could not settle causality, because its control group was already active [7].

## How to raise VO2max

- In healthy adults, steady endurance training raised VO2max by 4.9 mL/kg/min and intervals by 5.5, compared with controls [8].
- The Norwegian 4 × 4, four 4-minute bouts at 90–95 % of maximal heart rate with 3 easy minutes between, raised VO2max 7.2 % in eight weeks at three sessions a week [9]. A single 4-minute bout three times a week raised it 10 % in inactive men aged 35–45 [10].
- "Non-response" is a dose problem: with one hour a week 69 % of people did not improve; with four or five sessions everyone did [11].
- Zone 2 is not magic. A 2025 review found no evidence that it is the best intensity for fitness and argued that hard minutes matter more when weekly volume is low [12]. The working rule: most minutes easy, one or two hard sessions.

Skipping leg lifting does not mean skipping legs in cardio. VO2max depends mostly on how much blood the heart pumps, and it needs a large working muscle mass: arm cranking reaches only about 70 % of the oxygen uptake of cycling [13]. Use a bike, rower, incline treadmill or elliptical. Cardio after lifting does not blunt muscle growth or maximal strength [14].

## Finding zone 2 without a lab

Heart-rate formulas mislead individuals: in 100 adults the first lactate threshold sat anywhere from 60 % to 90 % of maximal heart rate [15]. The talk test does better. Recite a short passage while exercising: if speaking is comfortable you are below the first ventilatory threshold, and the stage where it becomes "not quite comfortable" matches the threshold [16][17]. The [heart-rate calculator](/fitness/nutrition) starts zone 2 at 60–65 % of heart-rate reserve and lets one talk-test session set its ceiling.

## Strength and muscle are survival signals

- In 139,691 adults from 17 countries, every 5 kg less grip strength was associated with 16 % higher all-cause mortality, a stronger predictor than systolic blood pressure [18].
- Across about two million people, high grip strength carried a hazard ratio of 0.69 and high knee-extension strength 0.86 [19].
- Muscle-strengthening activity is associated with 10–17 % lower mortality, with the largest effect at 30–60 minutes a week, and about 40 % lower when combined with aerobic activity [20].

## Bone needs heavy, local load

Bone adapts to large, fast strains where they occur. In the LIFTMOR trial, women with low bone mass trained 30 minutes twice a week: five sets of five deadlifts, squats and overhead presses above 80–85 % of 1RM, plus jumping chin-ups. After eight months their lumbar-spine density had risen 2.9 % against a 1.2 % loss in controls, and the only adverse event was one minor back spasm [21]. In older men with low bone mass the same approach raised spine density 4.1 % (controls 0.9 %), while a machine of near-maximal isometric pushes did not beat controls on bone [22]. High-intensity programs move spine density about 2.5 times more than moderate ones [23], and exercise cuts fractures by about a third [24].

The effect is local. The racket arm of tennis and squash players carries 8–16 % more bone mineral than the other arm, against 3–5 % in non-players [25]. Upper-body lifting added to leg and jumping work helped the spine, while hip gains came only from the lower-body work [26]. Walking protects the hip inconsistently and the spine not at all [27], and road cyclists often have low bone density [28]. Dieting costs bone too: weight loss lowers hip density by about 1–1.5 % [29], and in a 2025 trial in older adults with obesity neither resistance training nor a weighted vest prevented that loss [30].

## Carries, back extensions and balance

- Loaded carries demand a stiff trunk and a stable pelvis under load [31]. They load the standing spine and train grip, although no trial has measured their effect on bone.
- UK osteoporosis guidance recommends resistance training two to three days a week, at least 50 moderate impacts on most days and regular back-extension work [32]. Women who strengthened their back extensors had fewer vertebral fractures eight years after the program ended (1.6 % versus 4.3 % of vertebrae) [33].
- Balance is cheap to check: middle-aged and older adults who could not stand on one leg for 10 seconds had 84 % higher mortality over about seven years [34].

## The weekly longevity minimum

The WHO floor is 150–300 minutes of moderate or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity a week, plus strength work on two or more days [35]. Mortality kept falling up to 8,000–10,000 steps a day before 60 and 6,000–8,000 after [36]. Around three upper-body days that becomes:

| Component | Minimum, from week 1 | Target, from weeks 8–12 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lifting | the three A/B/C sessions | the same plus hip and leg lifts |
| Zone 2 | 2–3 × 10–15 min after lifting, 1 × 45–60 min | 3 × 15–20 min, 1 × 60–75 min |
| Intervals | one 4 × 4 session, about 40 min | one or two |
| All cardio | 150 moderate-equivalent min | 200–300 min |
| Steps a day | 8,000 (6,000 after 60) | 8,000–10,000 |
| Carries | 2 sessions | the same, heavier |
| Back extension | 2 sessions | the same, loaded |
| Impacts | optional 2 × 10 hops on 3 days | 5 × 10 on 4 or more days |
| Balance | 10-second one-leg test every 4 weeks | 2 × 30–60 s per leg on 2–3 days |
| Sleep | 7 h | 7–9 h |

Moderate-equivalent minutes count each vigorous minute twice [35]. The impacts follow the UK guidance [32] and the sleep floor the consensus that adults need at least seven hours [37]; the exact split is a practical default.

## Testing VO2max in the gym

| Test | How | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treadmill walk | 4 min brisk walk at 0 % grade, then 4 min at 5 %; heart rate over the last 30 s | ±4.9 mL/kg/min; submaximal, safe alone, ages 20–59 [38] |
| Bike ramp | +15 W a minute until the cadence fails | ±0.21 L/min in men; maximal, ages 20–70 [39] |
| 12-minute run | as far as possible on a measured route | maximal, for people who already run [40] |
| Heart-rate ratio | 15.3 × maximal ÷ resting heart rate | built in trained men; a rough day-0 guess [41] |
| Watch | the vendor's estimate | can be off by 10 mL/kg/min; trend only [42] |

Even good equations put only 51–59 % of people in the right fitness third [43]. So I log the raw result (time, watts, heart rate) and treat VO2max as derived. Between tests, heart rate at a fixed workload is the weekly signal: a four-week average about 5 bpm below baseline means fitter [44]. The [nutrition page](/fitness/nutrition) computes the 12-minute-run and heart-rate-ratio estimates.

## What this means for me

My program: three upper-body machine days with core work, in a fat-loss phase.

- **Cardio is covered.** Each session ends with a 12–15-minute finisher (zone 2 after A and C, short intervals after B), and a rest day gets the 4 × 4. I add one long easy walk or ride and keep 8,000 steps a day. If I start from little cardio, the 4 × 4 waits for four weeks of regular moderate sessions, as screening guidance tells inactive people to build up gradually [45].
- **Strength and muscle are covered** by the program itself, well beyond the 30–60 minutes a week linked with the lowest mortality; data on larger amounts are few and of very low certainty [20].
- **Bone is not covered, and I should be honest about it.** Seated, back-supported machines load my arms and shoulders. The seated cable row and the lower-back machine load the spine somewhat; the farmer's carry loads it more and is the only exercise that loads my hips at all, moderately, through loaded walking. Upper-body machine training does next to nothing for hip bone density, and the diet is taking some of it [29][30]. The program has no legs by default, so the fix is a set of additions I switch on myself on the [program page](/fitness/program); none of them is a template slot:
  - **Weeks 1–4:** the farmer's carry (day C) and the lower-back machine (day B) are already in; I add a [suitcase carry](/fitness/exercises/suitcase-carry) to day A and a [45° back extension](/fitness/exercises/back-extension-45) to day C; optional hops, 2 × 10 on three days.
  - **Weeks 5–12:** the legs add-on with the [leg press](/fitness/exercises/leg-press-45), 2–3 sets of 8–10 at RIR 2–3 once a week; optionally a standing [landmine press](/fitness/exercises/landmine-press) in place of the seated shoulder press; hops building to 50 a session.
  - **From week 13:** the [trap-bar deadlift](/fitness/exercises/trap-bar-deadlift) after two light technique weeks, building to five sets of five at 80–85 % of 1RM by about month 6, as in the trials above [21][22].
  - The leg press moves to week 1 if the cut runs longer than 12 weeks or past 10 % of body weight.
- **Tests:** a treadmill walk test now and every 8–12 weeks (same machine, same time of day), plus a weekly heart-rate check at a fixed setting. My targets: the 50th percentile for my age in the FRIEND registry first, then the 75th within one or two years [46]; below about 7.9 METs (27.7 mL/kg/min) counts as low fitness in the mortality studies [4].

## Caveats

- The links between fitness, strength and survival come from cohort studies: association, not proof [7].
- The bone trials studied older women and older men with low bone mass; in younger men with normal bone the effects are probably smaller, and the men's trial was only semi-randomised [22].
- Field-test estimates carry large individual errors; the trend over months matters more than one number [43].
- With heart, metabolic or kidney disease, or symptoms such as chest pain, unusual breathlessness, palpitations or fainting, get medical clearance before intervals or maximal tests, and stop a session that brings them on [45]. With osteoporosis, avoid deeply flexed spinal postures; after a vertebral fracture, keep impacts to brisk-walking level [32].
- This page explains evidence. It is not medical advice.
