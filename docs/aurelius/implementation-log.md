## 2026-11-12 — word alignment between wording and cited passages

Each lesson now carries a measured figure instead of decorative highlighting: a word-by-word cosine similarity matrix between the wording and the spans it cites, with precision, recall and F1 from greedy soft matching (BERTScore, Zhang et al. 2020, without idf weighting or baseline rescaling). `scripts/aurelius-alignment.py` computes it locally with `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` at a pinned revision (last hidden state, word-piece mean per word); no text leaves the machine and no paid request is made. It runs under the general Python environment, not the hash-locked Aurelius one, because it needs torch and transformers. Output: `content/aurelius/alignment.json`, keyed by revision id and carrying a hash of the study wording, spans and links; `tests/aurelius/alignment.test.mjs` fails when the artifact is stale and re-derives the published scores from the stored matrix. `AlignmentFigure` shows the scores, both texts tinted by similarity, a tap-to-inspect readout and the matrix; it is mounted in the reading view and under the synthesis explanation in the workbench, and hides itself when the wording has been edited since the run.

Limits: the model is small and close to lexical for archaic English (“examine” against “consider” scores 0.25), so low scores mean paraphrase, not error. The numbers describe wording, never support; evidence links remain manual. Regenerate after any change to study wording: `python scripts/aurelius-alignment.py`.
