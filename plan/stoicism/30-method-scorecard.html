Stoic Constitution · four extraction methods

Which method earns its score?

Two scorers marked individual source and article requirements present or absent. Extraction measures what the record preserves. Combination measures what reaches a usable two-source rule, including its limits.

What separated the methods

Complete thought + relations led extraction. In 5.6 it preserved who objected, how Marcus replied and what the reply allowed the reader to keep doing. In 5.20 it explicitly tied sensation reaching the mind to the unity of the body. All four formats omitted 5.16’s opening point about expecting wrongdoers never to do wrong.

Exact quotation tied for the highest article score. It was the only article in the reply-to-wrongdoing case to retain revenge as the original context. In the good-turn case, only the relation-labelled method’s article used cooperation inside the instruction about recognizing a kindness. On the impression and pain exercises, all methods passed every article check. These ceiling effects limit the ability to separate them on composition.

Under the default weights, short fragments and quotations tie overall. The two scorers individually reversed their order, so that lower-tier distinction is especially weak. Giving more than 50% weight to extraction moves fragments ahead of quotations; giving more than 50% to the finished articles reverses them. The relation-labelled method leads at every positive extraction weight and shares first place for articles alone.

What counted

Extraction · three passages
One 5.6 extract and two new extracts (5.16 and 5.20) per method. Each source and 65-word budget was the same across all four methods. Checks reward preserving specific source claims, speakers, scope and conditions.

Combination · four passage pairs
Four articles per method. Checks reward the contributions of each passage, source scope and a connection that applies to the same exercise or decision. Scorers judged the article itself, so a clarification in a separate note cannot earn it a point.

The source set comprises public Casaubon passages. The initial rubric was retrospective for the original twelve articles and four 5.6 extracts. For the added adversity pair, all extraction and combination checks and the weighting rule were written before generating any new text. The two new passages were selected because they were the remaining unused passages in the eight-passage public pilot.

One Codex writer saw all method instructions. Each new article used the corresponding two extracts under a shared 45-word limit; the writer had seen the source. The earlier two short-pair trials used editor-prepared input packets. All 12 extracts and 16 articles met the applicable word limits. Two Codex scorers then applied the same binary rubric separately. Only the second scorer received anonymous IDs and no method key. Formatting can reveal a method. This is a controlled pilot, not a human preference study or a comparison between LLM providers.

Agreement and arithmetic

The scorers agreed on 125 of 128 binary decisions (97.7%). A disagreement earns half a point by averaging the two ratings. Agreement measures repeatability between these scorers.

Convert each sample to 100 × earned points / available checks. Average the three extraction percentages and the four article percentages separately. The default overall score is the arithmetic mean of those two dimension scores. Display rounds to one decimal after ranking at full precision.

Casaubon 5.6

Such there be, who when they have done a good turn to any, are ready to set them on the score for it, and to require retaliation. Others there be, who though they stand not upon retaliation, to require any, yet they think with themselves nevertheless, that such a one is their debtor, and they know as their word is what they have done. Others again there be, who when they have done any such thing, do not so much as know what they have done; but are like unto the vine, which beareth her grapes, and when once she hath borne her own proper fruit, is contented and seeks for no further recompense. As a horse after a race, and a hunting dog when he hath hunted, and a bee when she hath made her honey, look not for applause and commendation; so neither doth that man that rightly doth understand his own nature when he hath done a good turn: but from one doth proceed to do another, even as the vine after she hath once borne fruit in her own proper season, is ready for another time. Thou therefore must be one of them, who what they do, barely do it without any further thought, and are in a manner insensible of what they do. 'Nay but,' will some reply perchance, 'this very thing a rational man is bound unto, to understand what it is, that he doeth.' For it is the property, say they, of one that is naturally sociable, to be sensible, that he doth operate sociably: nay, and to desire, that the party him self that is sociably dealt with, should be sensible of it too. I answer, That which thou sayest is true indeed, but the true meaning of that which is said, thou dost not understand. And therefore art thou one of those first, whom I mentioned. For they also are led by a probable appearance of reason. But if thou dost desire to understand truly what it is that is said, fear not that thou shalt therefore give over any sociable action.

Casaubon 5.16

To desire things impossible is the part of a mad man. But it is a thing impossible, that wicked man should not commit some such things. Neither doth anything happen to any man, which in the ordinary course of nature as natural unto him doth not happen. Again, the same things happen unto others also. And truly, if either he that is ignorant that such a thing hath happened unto him, or he that is ambitious to be commended for his magnanimity, can be patient, and is not grieved: is it not a grievous thing, that either ignorance, or a vain desire to please and to be commended, should be more powerful and effectual than true prudence? As for the things themselves, they touch not the soul, neither can they have any access unto it: neither can they of themselves any ways either affect it, or move it. For she herself alone can affect and move herself, and according as the dogmata and opinions are, which she doth vouchsafe herself; so are those things which, as accessories, have any co-existence with her.

Casaubon 5.20

Let not that chief commanding part of thy soul be ever subject to any variation through any corporal either pain or pleasure, neither suffer it to be mixed with these, but let it both circumscribe itself, and confine those affections to their own proper parts and members. But if at any time they do reflect and rebound upon the mind and understanding (as in an united and compacted body it must needs;) then must thou not go about to resist sense and feeling, it being natural. However let not thy understanding to this natural sense and feeling, which whether unto our flesh pleasant or painful, is unto us nothing properly, add an opinion of either good or bad and all is well.

Casaubon 9.13

Extraction · Good turns
  1. Explicitly identifies demanding repayment or openly keeping score for a good turn. Labels and period wording count.
  2. Explicitly identifies privately treating the recipient as a debtor.
  3. Uses the vine as the model for giving.
  4. Links doing another good turn with seeking no recompense. Both elements must survive.
  5. Attributes to an objector the view that an agent should recognize what they are doing. Labels count.
  6. Preserves the social extension of the objection that the recipient too should recognize the act.
  7. Shows Marcus both conceding a point and correcting the interpretation. Either alone is insufficient.
  8. Preserves the assurance in the reply that understanding the advice allows continued social action.

Topic + exact quotation

Repayment “to set them on the score for it, and to require retaliation.” Private reckoning “such a one is their debtor” Renewal “but from one doth proceed to do another” Objection “a rational man is bound unto, to understand what it is, that he doeth.” Assurance “fear not that thou shalt therefore give over any sociable action.”
