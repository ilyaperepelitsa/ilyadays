# Curated concepts, overlap and conceptual relationships

## Separate objects

A computational cluster run is a reproducible geometric proposal. A concept ontology is an editorial structure with definitions, inclusion/exclusion criteria, review history and explicit memberships. They must have separate records and lifecycle operations.

A cluster tree can propose nested groups. It does not supply overlapping memberships or certify that all grouped passages make the same claim. A rerun must not rename or delete the user’s concepts merely because the geometry changed.

## Concept proposal dossier

Each proposed concept includes a name, definition, representative passages, inclusion criteria, exclusion criteria, borderline cases, candidate parent concepts and relevant tensions. Record the run/model/manual origin, exact input revisions and review state.

Definitions should discriminate. “Life” and “being good” may be too broad to guide reconstruction. A concept should help the editor decide why a passage belongs and what apparently similar material does not belong. A broad organizational parent can exist without being treated as one substantive teaching.

## Membership model

Represent memberships as explicit many-to-many relationships from passage or proposition revisions to concept revisions. Store rationale, provisional relevance/ranking, assessor/run origin and review state. Source spans can explain which part of a multi-idea passage motivates membership.

A passage can belong to several concepts for different reasons. A proposition can refine membership more precisely than its parent passage. Unassigned passages and singleton concepts remain legal. Do not force unusual material into a large category merely to make the map visually tidy.

Relevance scores are ranking aids unless calibrated. Do not label a number a probability because it falls between zero and one. Human acceptance should preserve the rationale and evidence inspected rather than only a thresholded score.

## Relationship vocabulary

Keep at least these editorial distinctions visible:

| Relationship | Meaning | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Shared vocabulary | Similar terms appear | Same subject or same claim |
| Shared subject | Passages concern a common topic | Agreement |
| Shared claim | Their assertions closely align | Identical scope or justification |
| Shared practical implication | They can guide a similar action | Same metaphysical premise |
| Qualification | One limits another interpretation | Rejection of every related claim |
| Disagreement/tension | Claims resist easy reconciliation | A software import or clustering error |

Do not merge these automatically. A concept view should reveal supporting, qualifying and conflicting material rather than only nearest neighbors that make the concept look coherent.

## Hierarchy and associations

Allow multiple parents in the curated hierarchy, with cycle detection for parent relationships. Keep non-hierarchical associations separate; reciprocal associations are allowed where meaningful. Derivation lineage remains a different acyclic graph over immutable claims/revisions.

A parent change is an editorial decision with history. It need not invalidate an exact source anchor. A changed concept definition can make memberships stale and require review without automatically invalidating a separately reviewed conclusion whose evidence has not changed.

## Ontology editing

Support rename/definition revision, merge/split proposals, parent changes, manual membership addition/removal, explicit exclusions, alternative classifications and locks. Merges should preserve prior identities or redirects and review history. Splits should create candidates requiring their own membership review rather than duplicating approval indiscriminately.

A locked concept can receive new proposals alongside it. It cannot be overwritten by a model-assisted labeling pass. Rejected suggestions remain distinguishable from unseen material so repeated runs do not keep presenting the same rejected proposal without explanation.

## Discovery-to-writing workflow

A user selects a computational cluster and inspects representatives, outliers and shared vocabulary. They propose or select a curated concept, review its definition and add memberships. They then inspect actual propositions before drafting a teaching.

No “accept cluster as principle” shortcut should bypass claims and evidence. A cluster with disagreement can be useful as a research group while producing several incompatible or qualified conclusions.

## Evaluation

Use passages with multiple ideas, near-neighbor disagreement, rare vocabulary, singleton claims and different practical implications. Verify that overlap is preserved and that a concept’s criteria actually distinguish members from plausible non-members.

Track coverage of concepts and unassigned material separately from redundancy. A larger cluster is not necessarily a more important or more accurate concept. Do not optimize the ontology only for large balanced groups.

## Completion gate

One source passage can belong to several concepts without losing identity, accepted editorial decisions survive new cluster runs, definitions remain reviewable, and the user can inspect relevant tensions. The ontology helps reconstruct claims instead of substituting a preselected Stoic taxonomy for the text.
