Extraction quality · Casaubon 5.6

Which parts of an argument survive?

Marcus distinguishes demanding repayment from privately keeping score. He uses a vine to illustrate doing another good turn. An objector defends awareness of sociable action. Marcus acknowledges the point, disputes the reading and closes with an assurance about continuing to act sociably.

Topic + exact quotation · 57 words
Repayment “to set them on the score for it, and to require retaliation.”
Private reckoning “such a one is their debtor”
Renewal “but from one doth proceed to do another”
Objection “a rational man is bound unto, to understand what it is, that he doeth.”
Assurance “fear not that thou shalt therefore give over any sociable action.”

The renewal and assurance are quoted. The vine, lack of reward, social dimension of the objection and author’s concession disappear.

Role + short fragment · 64 words
Demand “to require retaliation”
Unspoken claim “such a one is their debtor”
Model “the vine, which beareth her grapes” → disposition “seeks for no further recompense” → practice “from one doth proceed to do another”
Objector’s duty “to understand what it is, that he doeth”
Marcus’s concession “true indeed” → correction “the true meaning of that which is said” / “thou dost not understand”

The vine, reward and next deed remain linked. The objector’s duty is generic, and the reply ends before the assurance about continued social action.

Complete thought · 61 words
Marcus distinguishes those who demand repayment from those who silently count the recipient as a debtor. He urges doing the next kindness as a vine bears its next crop, seeking no reward. An objector says a social being should know it acts socially and want the recipient to know too. Marcus accepts the point but says the objector misunderstands the advice.

The objection and its speaker are clear. The account ends at the misunderstanding and loses the assurance.

Complete thought + relations · 61 words
Distinction (Marcus): Some demand repayment. Others privately count a debt.
Analogy (Marcus): As the vine bears fruit again without reward, do one kindness and proceed to another.
Objection: A social being should recognize its social act and want the recipient to recognize it.
Concession and reply (Marcus): This is true, but the objector misreads the advice. Understanding it preserves social action.

All four distinctions are recoverable. “This is true” still leaves the extent of the concession open; the extract should not be read as an endorsement of wanting recognition.

Read the complete source · Casaubon 5.6
Such there be, who when they have done a good turn to any, are ready to set them on the score for it, and to require retaliation. Others there be, who though they stand not upon retaliation, to require any, yet they think with themselves nevertheless, that such a one is their debtor, and they know as their word is what they have done. Others again there be, who when they have done any such thing, do not so much as know what they have done; but are like unto the vine, which beareth her grapes, and when once she hath borne her own proper fruit, is contented and seeks for no further recompense. As a horse after a race, and a hunting dog when he hath hunted, and a bee when she hath made her honey, look not for applause and commendation; so neither doth that man that rightly doth understand his own nature when he hath done a good turn: but from one doth proceed to do another, even as the vine after she hath once borne fruit in her own proper season, is ready for another time. Thou therefore must be one of them, who what they do, barely do it without any further thought, and are in a manner insensible of what they do. 'Nay but,' will some reply perchance, 'this very thing a rational man is bound unto, to understand what it is, that he doeth.' For it is the property, say they, of one that is naturally sociable, to be sensible, that he doth operate sociably: nay, and to desire, that the party him self that is sociably dealt with, should be sensible of it too. I answer, That which thou sayest is true indeed, but the true meaning of that which is said, thou dost not understand. And therefore art thou one of those first, whom I mentioned. For they also are led by a probable appearance of reason. But if thou dost desire to understand truly what it is that is said, fear not that thou shalt therefore give over any sociable action.

Why it mattered. In the complete-thought version, the reader reaches “the objector misunderstands the advice” and stops. The relation-labelled version keeps the rest of the reply: the practice still leaves room for deliberate social action. A constitution article derived from the shorter account could leave that tension unresolved.

End-to-end check · the same 5.6 records + one fixed partner

Do those differences reach the article?

We handed each record above back to the writer with the same excerpt from Casaubon 1.15, starting “for we are all born to be fellow-workers.” The task was to write a first-person article on doing a good turn in shared life, in at most 45 words. Every version used both sources.

Topic + exact quotation

I was born to work with others. When I do someone a good turn, I will neither ask for repayment nor count that person as my debtor. I will go on to do another.

5.6 supplies the two kinds of debt and continuation; 1.15 supplies shared work. The writer makes the debt descriptions into refusals. Removing the first sentence leaves the rest of the rule intact.

Role + short fragment

I was born to work alongside others. Having done one good turn, I will take the grapevine as my model. I will seek no recompense and go on to do another.

The chain model → disposition → practice carries the vine into the article. The article drops the private ledger although the extraction contains it. Shared work mainly serves as the opening frame.

Complete thought

I was born to work with others. When I help someone, I will neither demand repayment nor silently count what they owe me. Like a vine bearing its next crop, I will turn to the next kindness without seeking a reward.

The article retains the vine and both kinds of repayment. “What they owe me” risks presupposing a debt. This packet contains the objection about awareness, but the draft leaves it unused.

Complete thought + relations

I was born to work with others. I can recognize a kindness as part of that work without privately counting a debt. As the vine bears fruit again, I will do the next kindness without asking for repayment.

“As part of that work” brings 1.15 into the substance of the rule. The writer grounds recognition in the objection plus the concession and labels the connection to private bookkeeping editorial. The exact scope of Marcus’s concession is still debatable.

Where I see a better combination. The relation-labelled draft distinguishes knowing that I am helping from privately counting a debt. It uses shared work to explain what I can recognize. In the other three drafts, shared work mainly introduces a rule already available from 5.6. The complete-thought packet also contained material for the richer reading. The difference in these drafts therefore shows a useful possibility, without showing that typed relations were necessary. The richer article also asks the editor to endorse a particular interpretation of the concession.

Combination quality · Casaubon 8.12 + 9.13

Make an exercise for meeting an impression

Each packet below was a different representation of the same pair. The same reader received the same assignment each time. Open a card to see the inputs behind the article.

Topic + exact quotation

Extract supplied to the writer

[Attention · 8.12] As every fancy and imagination presents itself unto thee, consider (if it be possible) the true nature, and the proper qualities of it, and reason with thyself about it.

[Judgment · 9.13] The things themselves that affect us, they stand without doors, neither knowing anything themselves nor able to utter anything unto others concerning themselves. What then is it, that passeth verdict on them? The understanding.

When an impression comes to me, I ask, if I can, what it is and what qualities belong to it. As I reason, I remember that my understanding gives the verdict.

The reminder and its timing are editorial. The condition and the identity of the judge survive.

Role + short fragment

Extract supplied to the writer

[8.12] Occasion “As every fancy and imagination presents itself unto thee” · Mental act “consider” · Qualification “(if it be possible)” → act · Object “the true nature, and the proper qualities of it” → act · Method “reason with thyself about it” → act

[9.13] Question “What then is it, that passeth verdict on them?” · Answer “The understanding.” → question

When an impression comes to me, I consider its nature and proper qualities, if possible, by reasoning with myself about it. I ask who gives the verdict and answer that my understanding does.

“By reasoning” commits the article to the method reading. The reader flags the missing antecedent of “them” in 9.13.

Complete thought

Extract supplied to the writer

[8.12] Whenever an impression presents itself, consider its nature and proper qualities if possible, and reason inwardly about it.

[9.13] External things cannot speak about themselves; the understanding passes the verdict on them.

When an impression comes, I consider its nature and qualities if I can, and reason about it inwardly. I remind myself that the thing cannot speak for itself and that my understanding judges it.

Keeps the limit, inward reasoning, and the source’s observation that the thing cannot speak. Recalling that point during the exercise is editorial.

Complete thought + relations

Extract supplied to the writer

[8.12] Whenever an impression presents itself, consider its nature and proper qualities if possible, and reason inwardly about it.

[9.13] External things cannot speak about themselves; the understanding passes the verdict on them.

The impression is the object of “consider”. “If possible” qualifies “consider”. Reasoning can be read as a coordinated act or method. In 9.13 “the understanding” answers who gives the verdict.

When an impression presents itself, I consider, if possible, its nature and proper qualities, and I reason about it inwardly. Then I ask who gives the verdict and answer that my understanding does.

The links help recover the scope of “if possible.” The new word “Then” imposes a sequence. The reader identifies it as an addition.

Result. All four drafts retained a qualified act of examination and identified the understanding as judge. In the companion check, all four rejected the idea that the passages supply two independent tests of soundness. Short fragments lost the referent of “them.” The relation-labelled version documented an ambiguous reading but added a sequence to the prose. The writer identified that sequence as editorial.

Combination quality · Casaubon 6.5 + 6.49

Choose a reply to someone who wronged me

This case tests two distinctions: the revenge maxim’s scope, and the direction of the claim about hive and bee. Applying that claim to a reply within a human community is an editorial step.

Topic + exact quotation

Extract supplied to the writer

[Revenge · 6.5] The best kind of revenge is, not to become like unto them.

[Common good · 6.49] That which is not good for the bee-hive, cannot be good for the bee.

If I want revenge, I choose a reply that keeps me from becoming like the person who wronged me. I cannot count a reply as good for me if it is no good for us.

The opening keeps revenge as the context. Applying hive and bee to “us” is an editorial analogy.

Role + short fragment

Extract supplied to the writer

[6.5] Criterion “not to become like unto them”

[6.49] Condition “That which is not good for the bee-hive” · Consequence “cannot be good for the bee.” → condition

When someone wrongs me, I choose a reply that keeps me from becoming like them. If a reply is no good for my community, I cannot count it as good for me.

The sentence sounds complete. The reader reports having to get the offender from the assignment and cannot recover what the source originally evaluated.

Complete thought

Extract supplied to the writer

[6.5] Refusing to become like the offender is the best kind of revenge.

[6.49] What is not good for the hive cannot be good for the bee.

When I am wronged, I choose a reply that keeps me from becoming like the offender. Before calling it good for me, I ask whether it is good for my community.

Preserves the necessary condition. Extending revenge to replies generally and applying the analogy are editorial steps, acknowledged by the reader.

Complete thought + relations

Extract supplied to the writer

[6.5] Refusing to become like the offender is the best kind of revenge.

[6.49] What is not good for the hive cannot be good for the bee.

“Best kind of” evaluates revenge in 6.5. 6.49 is a one-way claim linking the good of hive and bee. The hive and bee are the literal terms of the comparison.

When I choose my reply, I ask whether I would be doing what I blame the offender for. If a reply is no good for my community, I rule it out as good for me.

The reader turns resemblance into doing what one blames the offender for. That is a particular interpretation of imitation. The group condition keeps the correct direction.

Result. All four outputs refused the converse when asked whether benefiting the community makes a reply good for the individual. Only the quotation-based article explicitly opens with revenge. Both complete-thought packets actually contained that frame, so their broader articles illustrate a loss during composition. The short-fragment packet had already lost it during extraction.

How I would use the four methods

MethodUse in the projectWatch for
Topic + exact quotationIndex passages and keep a full sentence accessible. A short aphorism can already be an excellent extract.A tight word budget can preserve striking words and lose the argument around them.
Role + short fragmentHighlight a condition, negation, speaker change or object inside a complete thought.Pronouns, modal force and the scope of a maxim can disappear while the sentence assembled from the tags still sounds fluent.
Complete thoughtUse as the ordinary unit to pass to a composer, with exact source anchors.A paraphrase can end too early. Both complete-thought judgment packets also omit the source’s statement that external things know nothing themselves.
Complete thought + relationsAdd “qualifies,” “answers,” “illustrates” or “objects to” where it changes the reading.The writer can ignore a preserved qualifier or add a sequence. Check the resulting article against the source.

Provisional choice. store the original sentence and a complete thought together. Add a relation when removing it would change who is speaking, when the rule applies, what follows, or what stays unresolved. During composition, trace the final scope and the editor’s addition separately.

What was compared

The trial included an extraction task, a downstream comparison, and an end-to-end check. For extraction, a second Codex session received Casaubon 5.6 and four format instructions, each limited to 65 words. The same session returned one record per format. For composition, the editor supplied four packets for each of two other pairs. One downstream Codex reader produced eight articles, a map to the local evidence, and a response to the case’s inference check. The subsequent end-to-end check used the four 5.6 records and one fixed excerpt from 1.15 to produce four more articles. The same reader had previously seen the full 5.6 passage. It was instructed to rely only on the current record and to map the drafted clauses to it. The dossier reproduces all twelve articles; packet IDs and typography are standardized for reading.

Each composition request allowed 35 words for the article, a separate explanation of editorial additions, and a refusal if a join failed. The first three conditions were presented together under neutral packet names; the short-fragment condition followed. The writer saw multiple formats, and the editor who prepared the packets judged the results. The composer had no separate source lookup, but seeing the other packets can cue missing context. The two short quotation packets contained the full selected passages, while the other packets compressed or annotated them. The comparison therefore measures these specimens, without isolating format from length, selection, order or reader preference.

The four extraction checks were set before the 5.6 records were requested. They deliberately test argument preservation. The article checks examined scope, contribution from both passages, named editorial additions and refusal of an unsupported inference. A source detail can be absent from a 35-word article without being a defect. There was no independent human preference vote, unseen corpus sample, or comparison between LLM providers.
